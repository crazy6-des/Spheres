01 — The mechanism

Per-sphere fee markets, so nobody subsidises a sphere they never touch.

A single global pool means your bond pays for blocks you have no stake in. Spheres each carry their own fee market: you are paid by the workload you underwrite, and a sphere nobody uses earns close to nothing. That is the correct signal, and it is why the rates in §4 differ by more than a rounding error.

Bonding is a signature, not a transaction. Gas is zero. The queue only starts when you ask to leave.

1

Pick a sphere

Nine exist. Four are sponsor-gated and pay more; five are open and pay less. Every rate is published in §4.

2

Sign the bond

Gasless, instant. Your stake weights that sphere's proposer sortition — you are underwriting its blocks specifically.

3

Accrue per block

Yield accrues on principal every block. Claiming is a signature and never starts the queue.

4

Rebond freely

Move between spheres without unbonding. Leave a sphere with degrading uptime in one signature.

02 — The decomposition

Four sources. Three are structural. One is labelled temporary.

2.1 Why the surplus is the biggest line

On a constant-product AMM, the pool's liquidity providers collect 0.30% and that fee is their compensation for being the counterparty — for holding inventory that moves against them. Spheres have no LPs. The staker occupies that seat. Solvers bid for the right to fill; everything above the floor price is passed through in full because there is no LP to pay it to.

This is also why the venue cut is 0.04% and not 0.30%. A venue that keeps the spread cannot also pay a bond out of it. The thin cut is the mechanism, not a discount.

2.2 Why sealing the auction raised the figure

Epoch 06 ran an open auction and solvers shaded their bids to just above the incumbent. Epoch 07 seals it: shading now loses the win, so bids converge toward true value. Surplus rose from 3.1% APY realised to 14.1% APY forward on a mechanism change, not on optimism. That is the single largest jump in the table and it is the one to interrogate.

2.3 Why the subsidy is the smallest line

It is 8 of 35 and it decays to zero across eight epochs. If it were half the number the whole thing would read as a farm. At 8% it is a rounding error on the decision — so the decision has to stand on the other 27.

2.4 Nothing is paid in new coins

Inflation is 2M/yr on a 100M float and none of it routes to stakers. A farm pays you in something it prints; this pays you in fees it collected, which is why the rate cannot be diluted away and also why it cannot be propped up.

The price of the rateSeven days of illiquidity, not skippable, no fast lane — and slashing reaches bonded principal. Free money with no lock and no downside is the thing that smells. This has both, and that is why the number can exist.

7.1 The 5% epoch cap is absolute. A catastrophic epoch costs a twentieth and no more; two in a row is 10%. At a 27% structural rate a single bad epoch is repaid in roughly ten weeks.

7.2 Slashes compute at settlement and are visible before execution. You can rebond off a sphere in the gap between the two.

7.3 Proceeds split 50/50 between the reserve and the reporter, so detection is paid and not purely altruistic.

What is not at riskUnbonded SPHERE, and yield already claimed. Slashing reaches bonded principal only — claim regularly if a sphere's uptime looks soft, since claimed yield cannot be reached.
08 — Objections

In the order they arrive

"35% is a farm number"

27% is structural and none of it is printed. §2.4: no inflation routes to stakers. A farm pays you in something it mints; this pays you in fees it collected.

"So what happens at low volume"

§3 says it plainly: at a quarter of today's volume the structural rate is 10.9%. Above every flexible CEX rate. The tripwire is published.

"Why is the surplus line bigger than the fee line"

Because the staker replaced the LP. §2.1 explains the substitution and why the venue cut is 0.04% instead of 0.30%.

"Seven days is too long"

Then bond a fraction. Nothing forces a full balance and unbonded SPHERE still swaps at 0.04%. Ladder it a third at a time.

"What if the subsidy ends while I'm in"

The rate steps to the structural column and the changelog announces it before the step. Principal is untouched; the calculator's third option shows the exact cost.

"What if the sphere dies"

Rebond without unbonding. You drop to the new sphere's rate and lose nothing but the spread.

"Can they slash me for nothing"

Slashing reaches proposers, not stakers directly. The 5% epoch cap bounds the pass-through and slashes are visible pre-execution.

"Is this a security"

No. SPHERE pays fees and bonds work. No equity, no dividend, no claim on assets. §14 of the rules.

"Do I need a seat to bond"

No. Staking is open in every region. The seat program and staking are separate — restricted regions can still bond.
