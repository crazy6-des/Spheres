§1

Overview

Spheres is a modular execution layer for settlement-agnostic rollups. Execution shards into named spheres, each with its own proposer set and its own fee market, and all of them settle to a shared base with blob data availability.

There is no AMM. Orders are signed intents auctioned to competing solvers in a sealed-bid round; the winning solver carries slippage on its own book and returns any surplus above the floor price to the user. The venue's cut is 0.04% flat and is the only cut.

SPHERE is the utility token. It pays fees, bonds proposers, and bonds solvers. It is not a claim on assets, revenue or equity. Stakers are paid from fee revenue and auction surplus — not from inflation — which is why §9 spends most of its length qualifying the rate rather than advertising it. The epoch pool itself is denominated and paid in USDT, so claiming carries no token exposure.

Status

Epoch 06 closed 4 November. Epoch 07 is the fourth of four cohort epochs and the last before permissionless execution. Current limitations are in §14 and include two that matter.

Read this before the architectureForced inclusion is not live (§14.1). The sequencer is single-leader with hot standby (§14.2). Both are scheduled and neither is hidden. If either is disqualifying for you, that is a legitimate reason to wait for epoch 08.

§4

Spheres

A sphere is an execution shard with a name, a proposer set, and a fee market of its own. Stake follows the sphere: bonding to a sphere weights your capital behind that sphere's blocks and earns that sphere's fee revenue specifically.

This is the design decision the whole economics rests on. A single global fee pool means your bond subsidises blocks you never touch. Per-sphere markets mean you are paid by the workload you actually underwrite — and a sphere nobody uses earns close to nothing, which is the correct signal.

Class

Sponsored4 spheres

Proposer sets rotate per epoch by stake-weighted sortition with a 32-seat cap, so no operator holds more than one seat per sphere per epoch regardless of stake. The cap is what stops a large staker from becoming the sphere.

The honest tradeSponsored spheres pay better and are gatekept. In epoch 07 four of nine spheres are sponsored, so the advertised ceiling requires a sponsor's admission. An open-sphere-only staker sits below the ceiling and the calculator on /ecosystem says so.
§5

Intent router

An intent is a signed statement of an acceptable outcome, not a transaction. It carries sell token, buy token, limit price, expiry, and a nonce. Nothing is broadcast against a curve, so there is no curve for a searcher to move first.

struct Intent { address owner; address sellToken; address buyToken; uint256 sellAmount; uint256 minBuyAmount; // the limit; fill below this reverts uint48 expiry; // max 20 blocks uint256 nonce; // single-use, per owner bytes signature; }

Intents are submitted to a public mempool and are not hidden from solvers — hiding them is §14.4. Solvers see the intent; they do not see each other's bids, which is the part that matters for price.

An unfilled intent expires without cost. There is no revert gas because there is no transaction to revert. The failure mode of a bad quote is that nothing happens.

§6

Solver auction

Sealed bid, 12-block window, one winner. Bids close at the boundary and open together; the highest bid above the floor wins and pays its bid, not the second price.

This is a first-price sealed bid and it is the reason the surplus figure moved between epochs. In epoch 06 the auction was open and solvers shaded to just above the incumbent. Epoch 07 seals it, so shading costs the win and bids converge upward — which is why §9 assumes 1.08%/month of surplus forward against 0.22% realised. The increase is a mechanism change, not optimism.

Twelve solvers are approved in epoch 07 and the set is permissioned (§14.3). Admission is a bond, a signed operating agreement, and a liveness test over 10,000 blocks. There is no application fee and no vote.

§7

Settlement and data availability

Batches post to the base every 12 minutes with a KZG commitment per blob. Sphere state roots are verified against the settled commitment before the root is accepted. A root with no available blob is not accepted, which is the whole point of paying for DA rather than trusting a mirror.

L1 withdrawals take 7 days, matching the proof window and the unbond queue. Both are the same number on purpose: you never wait for one clock and not the other.

Reorg handling: a reorg deeper than the batch is resolved by re-execution from the last settled root. User intents in the orphaned range are re-auctioned, not dropped, and keep their original nonce so a duplicate fill reverts.

The two numbers to hold ontoSubsidy is 0.52 of the 2.53 and it dies. Sponsorship needs a sponsor's admission. 2.01%/month is the figure that survives both, and it is still conditional on volume we do not control. If fee volume halves, so does the rate, and no parameter change can stop that.

Accrual and exit

Yield accrues per block on bonded principal and compounds at claim. Claiming is a signature and does not touch principal or start the queue. Unbonding is 7 days, not skippable, no fast lane — it is the price of the rate and the reason the rate can exist.

Subsidy drawdown publishes weekly against the funding address. When it reaches zero the rate steps down and the changelog records it in advance of the step.

Compounding convention

Every monthly figure in the table is the APY divided by compounding, not by twelve. 35.0% APY compounds to 2.53%/month and 1.35× over a year; 27.0% to 2.01%/month and 1.27×. Dividing the annual rate by twelve understates the monthly yield and the two conventions were mixed in v0.9.0 — corrected in v0.9.2, and the calculator on /ecosystem uses the compounding convention.

Epoch cap is 5% of bond per epoch regardless of offence count, so a bad epoch cannot zero a proposer. Slashes are computed at settlement and are visible before they execute.

Slash proceeds are split 50/50 between the reserve and the reporting party. Reporting is paid, so detection is not purely altruistic.

§14

Known limitations

Seven, worst first, with what each costs you right now. If a limitation is on this list it is real, it is not being quietly fixed, and it has a date or it does not ship.

1

No forced inclusion

A censoring sequencer can hold your transaction for up to 12 minutes with no on-chain escape hatch. Cost: you cannot force a fill through the base layer today.

Epoch 08 · design frozen, unaudited
2

Single-leader sequencer

One leader at a time with a hot standby. Failover is fast and the operator is still a single point of ordering. Cost: one operator's judgement, every block.

Epoch 09 · leader rotation designed
3

Permissioned solver set

Twelve approved solvers. Good execution, thin long tail, and admission is ours to grant. Cost: you cannot be a solver without our approval.

Epoch 08 · open with bond
4

Intent mempool is public

Intents are visible before fill, so a fast backrunner can trade ahead of a large one. Cost: large intents get worse fills than they should.

Epoch 08 · commit-reveal
5

No light client

Verification requires an archive node or trusting an RPC. Cost: the §2 table assumes you have an RPC you trust.

Epoch 09
6

7-day L1 withdrawal

Proof window and unbond queue are the same clock by design. Cost: a week to base, every time.

not changing
7

No smart accounts

No EIP-4337, no session-key accounts, no social recovery. Cost: an EOA, or a Safe you set up yourself.

Epoch 09
What is not on this listThings that work: settlement, DA, slashing, unbonding, the reserve, the auction, Permit2. This list is short because the other list is long, not because we ran out of room.

§17

API

Read endpoints are open and unauthenticated. Write endpoints need a signed intent, not a key.

GET/v1/quote ?sell&buy&amount
Floor price and expected surplus for a size. Public, cached 1 block.
GET/v1/spheres
Sphere list with class, rate, and proposer count.
GET/v1/rate ?sphere
Current and trailing rate, subsidy share broken out.
GET/v1/bond ?address
Bonded amount, accrual, queue position if unbonding.
POST/v1/intent
Submit a signed intent. Returns an intent hash.
POST/v1/bond
Signed bond or unbond instruction.
DEL/v1/intent/:hash
Cancel before the auction window closes. Gasless.

Quote

// GET /v1/quote?sell=SPHERE&buy=USDT&amount=1000000000000000000 { "floor": "421200", // min acceptable, 6dp "expected": "421712", // with trailing surplus "surplus_bps": 12, "fee_bps": 4, "expiry_block": 23914818, "route": ["SPHERE", "USDT"] }

floor is what your minBuyAmount should be set to. expected is informational and not a promise — the auction decides.

Rate limitsQuote is 20 rps per IP, intent is 5 rps per address. Over-limit returns 429 with a Retry-After. There is no paid tier and no key to buy.
§18

Running a solver

Admission is closed in epoch 07 (§14.3) but the software is open and a local run against a fork is the admission test. Bond 50,000 SPHERE, pass 10,000 blocks of liveness, sign the operating agreement.

# fork from a settled root solverd init --network spheres --fork-block 23914600 # fund the bond from the operator key solverd bond --amount 50000 --key ./operator.key # dry run: 200 intents, no broadcast solverd simulate --intents 200 --report ./sim.json # live, one sphere, one block window solverd run --sphere open-3 --window 12 --max-exposure 40000

A solver must hold inventory in both legs of any pair it bids on. Bidding a pair you cannot fill is the fastest way to lose fill duty and the simulate report flags it before you go live.

Fill duty is 92% of wins per 1,000 blocks. Below it, priority drops for a full epoch and recovers only by holding the rate for another.
