01 — Mechanics

How a fill actually happens

Three moves, and the middle one is the only place a solver can touch anything. It is also the only place a solver can lose.

1

You declare

Pair, size and a minimum you will accept. That floor is binding — a solver that cannot beat it does not fill. No "0.5% slippage" box to guess at.

Free · signature only
2

Solvers bid

Independent solvers route across every venue they can reach and submit against your floor. They post their own inventory to compete. The spread goes to whoever is fastest and cheapest, not to the pool.

30s window · permissionless set
3

Best fill settles

One winner, one transaction, receipt with block and hash. Losers pay their own gas. If the window closes empty, the intent expires and your balance is untouched.

Gasless · instant receipt
02 — Why

An AMM is a price. An intent is a market.

On a curve you are trading against a formula that has already published its answer, and the mempool reads it before you land. Sandwiching is not an attack on the curve — it is the curve working as specified.

Here there is no formula to front-run, because there is no formula. The quote arrives after your intent is public and the winning solver is bound to it on-chain. Adversarial ordering stops being a tax and starts being a service fee.

Exposure

Your floor, or nothing

A fill below your minimum is invalid. You cannot be filled worse than you asked, only not filled at all.

Cost

0.04%, symmetrical

Charged on the fill, not on the attempt. Expired and rejected intents cost nothing.

Approval

Permit2, capped, expiring

Same canonical contract Uniswap has run since 2022. Capped at the intent, dead in thirty days, revocable.

Failure

Solvers carry it

Reverted fills, stale routes and bad inventory are the solver's gas. You see the failure, you do not pay for it.
