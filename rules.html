The rules, and the function behind them. Read the function.

Everything below evaluates against one block, returns a boolean or an integer, and writes the result to a merkle tree. There is no committee at first instance. Where this page and any other page disagree, this page wins — including the landing page, including anything we say on X.

If you only read two sections, read §4 and §5. They are the scoring function and the weights. They are published in full because a rule you cannot inspect is a promise, not a rule.

§1

Scope, precedence, definitions

1.1 These rules govern cohort 01 of Epoch 07 only. Later cohorts are governed by their own published version, which will carry a different number and a different snapshot.

1.2 Precedence. In any conflict, this order applies: (a) the on-chain evaluation at the snapshot block; (b) this page at version 1.0; (c) the documentation; (d) any other page, post, thread or message, including from staff. Where (a) and (b) disagree, (a) governs and we must publish a correction under §12.

1.3 Nothing on this page is an offer, a guarantee of a seat, or a representation of value. A seat exists only where an address satisfies §3 and §6 at the snapshot block and is confirmed under §8.

1.4 Defined terms carry the meaning in the glossary at the foot of this page and nowhere else. Where a term is not defined, the plain meaning applies, read narrowly.

§2

The snapshot

2.1 All five clauses in §3, all three scoring components in §4, and every sybil determination in §6 evaluate against state as of the close of block 23,914,806. State after that block is ignored, with two exceptions: claim submission (§10) and appeal (§12).

2.2 The block hash is published at freeze, in the epoch report and in the footer of this page. Anyone can re-run the function against an archive node at that height and arrive at the same number. That is the point of publishing §4.

2.3 Scores are computed once. They are not recomputed at claim, not refreshed, and not re-derived from later activity. A wallet that goes dormant on 10 December and one that trades through the window score identically.

Why a block, not a windowA window lets you watch the threshold and react to it. A block is a closed door: you either cleared the bar before it shut or you did not, and you can prove which without asking us. It also means the answer cannot be sold, timed, or softened for anyone after the fact.
§3

The five clauses

Each clause is a pure function of snapshot state returning true or false. All five must return true. The last column is whether a failing address can still change its answer before the block closes — and every one of those three is worth acting on tonight, not tomorrow.

Clause 3.4 is the expensive one. An unattested address caps at T3 at $1,000. The same address attested reaches T1 at $15,000. The gap between fixing 3.4 and not fixing it is $14,000, and it takes four minutes.

§4

Scoring, in full

This is the whole function. Four terms, three positive and one negative, integer output, maximum 1,000. It is reproduced here in the same form the evaluation service runs it. Weights are published because a hidden weight is where discretion hides.

// evaluated once, at block 23,914,806 fn score(addr) -> u16: // 1. tenure — 1.4 points per day, hard cap at 300 days t = min(420, floor(1.4 * days_since(first_outbound(addr)))) // 2. diversity — 30 per distinct verified contract, cap 11 d = min(330, 30 * distinct_contracts(addr, window=90d, verified=true)) // 3. volume — log-scaled on 90d median, cap $25,000 v = min(250, floor(250 * log10(1 + median_usd(addr, 90d)) / log10(1 + 25000))) // 4. penalty — sybil adjacency, cap 120 p = min(120, 60 * adjacency_signals(addr)) return clamp(t + d + v - p, 0, 1000)

Reproduced from epoch07/score.rs · commit 4b1c9ae · the same build the checker calls

4.1 Why the weights sit where they do

Tenure carries 42% because time is the one thing a farmer cannot manufacture retroactively. Diversity carries 33% because breadth of use is the closest cheap proxy for a human. Volume carries 25% because it is the easiest to fake and therefore the least trusted. An old quiet wallet beats a new loud one, structurally — not by editorial preference.

4.2 Median, not total

Volume uses the median of the 90-day distribution, not the sum. One $400,000 wash trade in a single block adds almost nothing. Twenty real swaps of $40 add a lot. A wallet that routes everything through one pair is measured on its typical day.

4.3 Verified contract, defined

A contract counts once, however many times it is called, and only if source is verified on the explorer and it is not a proxy to another counted contract. Self-deployed contracts count as verified, but deployments from the same deployer with byte-identical bytecode and no parameter divergence count once between them — nine tokens you minted yourself are diversity of one, not nine.

4.4 Adjacency signals

Each of the following contributes one signal, capped at two: shared first funder with ≥3 other addresses within 72 hours of first funding; near-identical transaction ordering across ≥5 transactions; contract deployment from the same deployer with byte-identical bytecode and no divergence in parameters. Two signals is a −120, which is a full tier in most bands.

4.5 Floor and ceiling

The clamp runs last. A heavily penalised wallet cannot score below zero, and no combination of bonuses exceeds 1,000. Tenure at the 300-day cap plus perfect diversity plus $25,000 median is exactly 1,000 and nothing scores above it.

4.6 Determinism

Two intermediate floats and nothing else: the tenure multiply and the volume logarithm. Both are computed in fixed-point at 6 decimal places, floored, and integer thereafter. No time-of-run dependency, no random seed. Two evaluators with the same archive node and the same commit produce identical bytes. If our number differs from yours, one of us has the wrong commit and §12 covers it.

4.7 Tie-breaks

Applied in order until the tie breaks: (a) greater tenure in days; (b) lower median transaction count; (c) lexicographically lower address, lowercase hex, as a string. The third is arbitrary and deliberately so — it is the only one that cannot be gamed in the final hour.

Do not take our word for the numberAn archive node at block 23,914,806, the commit above, and the function printed on this page are everything the evaluator has. Run it. Where your result and ours diverge, the divergence is the appeal — not the outcome.

5.1 A score below 300 does not seat. The gap between 299 and 300 is worth $1,000 and there is no rounding, no partial tier, and no discretionary seat.

5.2 The T1 tenure gate is a conjunction, not a disjunction. Score 940 on a 120-day wallet is T2. Tenure 400 days with a score of 700 is T2. Both conditions, or it falls through.

5.3 Settlement asset. The pool is USDT and settles in USDT, at the figure printed above. There is no conversion step, no reference rate applied at claim, and no spot exposure between registering and being paid. The $0.42 mark exists only because the reserve was provisioned out of SPHERE at that price; it governs internal accounting and the top-up test below, and it governs nothing about what arrives in your wallet.

5.4 If SPHERE trades below $0.30 on a thirty-day TWAP before the window opens, the treasury tops the pool back to par in USDT under §9.6. The top-up is a treasury obligation, not a haircut on your figure.

5.5 If a tier is undersubscribed at the snapshot, its remainder flows downward — T1 surplus to T2, then T2 to T3 — and per-seat figures are recomputed to keep the pool at exactly $3,000,000. Surplus never flows upward into a smaller tier and never returns to the treasury. The table above is the target, not a floor.

5.6 If a tier is oversubscribed, seats fill by score descending within that tier. A T2 address ranked 201st in a 200-seat tier is not demoted to T3 — it is unseated, and §10.9 applies.

§6

Sybil policy

6.1 A cluster is a connected component of the funding graph, computed over first-funding edges only, where two addresses join if one first-funded the other, or both were first-funded by the same address within 72 hours. Edges after first funding are ignored.

6.2 One seat per cluster. The highest-scoring member takes it. The rest are not rejected — they are shadowed, and a shadowed address becomes eligible automatically if the winner fails to claim inside the window.

6.3 The cluster is scored as a whole for clause 3.2. Five transactions spread across five shadowed wallets is five transactions, not twenty-five.

6.4 A wallet funded from a CEX hot wallet does not join every other wallet funded from it. Exchange hot wallets are on a denylist and are transparent to the graph; the edge runs to the withdrawal's originating deposit address where one is recoverable, and is dropped where it is not.

6.5 You may self-declare a cluster break by attestation where the graph is wrong — a shared funder who is an employer, a family member, an OTC desk. Declaration is reviewed under §12 and does not change a score, only a cluster assignment.

6.6 Shadowing is the one place where losing is not final. A shadowed address is already eligible; it is waiting on someone else's inaction, and roughly a tenth of shadowed addresses are expected to be promoted inside the window.

The honest versionSybil detection is the softest part of any allocation and we will get some of it wrong. The graph is published per address at claim so you can see exactly which edge joined you and contest that edge specifically — not the outcome, the edge.
§7

Identity attestation

7.1 Either path satisfies 3.4. Neither requires a document to be uploaded to us: the on-chain path is a signed message from the address, and the social path is a challenge string posted from a handle that meets 7.3.

7.2 The on-chain attestation is a SphAttest call carrying the signing address, a jurisdiction claim, and a timestamp. It is public, cheap, and revocable. Attestations created after the snapshot do not count, which is why the block closes before claims open.

7.3 A handle qualifies at 90 days of age, 50 followers and 20 posts, all measured at the snapshot. Newly minted accounts fail and the checker says so.

7.4 One attestation, one address. Attesting from a second address does not create a second seat — §6.2 applies.

7.5 The jurisdiction claim in 7.2 is what §11 reads. Where a claim is absent, region falls back to IP at verification time, which is weaker and can be contested under §12.

7.6 An attestation is revocable and survives the epoch. Revoking after the snapshot does not unseat you; revoking before it does.

§8

Registration and ordering

8.1 Registration opens 12 November 14:00 UTC and closes at the snapshot. It is free, needs no signature, and is not a reservation — registering does not hold a seat.

8.2 Seats are assigned at the snapshot from the full eligible set, by tier and then score. Registration order is a tiebreak only inside a tier that is already oversubscribed at equal score after §4.7.

8.3 The seat counter on the site is a projection from current state, not a ledger. It can fall. A counter that only rises is a counter nobody maintains.

8.4 The merkle root of all leaves is published at 12 Nov 06:00 UTC, eight hours before claims open, so the tree is auditable — and appealable under §12 — before anything can be claimed against it. Leaf order is address ascending, lowercase hex.

8.5 One seat per address, per cluster, per attestation. Multiple wallets from one person are permitted and expected; multiple seats from one person are not.

8.6 Publishing the root before the window is deliberate. It converts "trust us" into eight hours in which anyone can check their own leaf and object to it in public.

§9

Custody of the pool

9.1 The full $3,000,000 is deposited and locked 72 hours before the epoch opens — by 9 Nov 14:00 UTC — at the reserve address published in the docs and mirrored in the footer.

9.2 The reserve is a 4-of-7 multisig. Seven signers, three jurisdictions, HSM-held keys, no signer holding two. Four signatures move anything; no single operator, and no pair, can.

9.3 Signers are named in the docs with their jurisdiction. Two are external to the company.

9.4 The lock is a timelock contract, not a promise: releases require the lock to have expired or a 4-of-7 unlock, and both are visible on an explorer.

9.5 Reserve movements during the window are published line by line in the epoch report, with block height. There is no unannounced movement.

9.6 Top-up. If the TWAP check in §5.4 triggers, the top-up arrives in USDT at the same address, before the window opens, and is reported the same way. Top-up funds are not recoverable by the treasury once deposited.

9.7 Nothing in the pool is borrowed, deployed to a yield venue, or rehypothecated during the window. It sits.

§10

Claim, mechanics, forfeiture

10.1 Claims open 12 Nov 14:00 UTC and close 12 Dec 14:00 UTC. There is no grace period and no manual reopening.

10.2 25% releases at claim. 75% streams linearly over 90 days to the claiming address. The stream is transferable only as it vests.

10.3 The claim binds to the scored address. The merkle leaf is that address. Claiming to a different address is not reversible — check twice, then check the checksum.

10.4 Claim mechanics

The claim is a typed-data signature, EIP-712, against the claim contract. It carries your address, your tier, your amount, a single-use nonce and a deadline. A relayer submits it and pays the gas. You pay nothing to claim.

10.5 The claim itself needs no token allowance. USDT moves reserve → you, so there is nothing for you to approve in order to be paid. If you want to be armed for the router and the staking module afterwards, that is a separate, optional step: one capped approval of USDT to 0x000000000022D473030F116dDEE9F6B43aC78BA3 — Permit2, the canonical contract, identical on every chain, deployed since 2022. Capped at your allocation, self-expiring at thirty days, revocable at any moment.

10.6 That allowance is a permission, not a debit. It is the most the spender can ever move out of your wallet; it is not a ceiling on what we send you, and you are not charged it. Nothing moves at the approval step.

10.7 A receipt with block height and transaction hash is returned within one block of the relayer's submission. Keep the height — §14.3 makes it your tax record.

10.8 Forfeiture

10.8 A seat is forfeited if it is unclaimed at cutoff, if the address is later found to fail §3 or §6 on re-evaluation under §12, or if the claim signature is invalid at submission and not corrected inside the window.

10.9 Recycling. Forfeited and shadowed seats do not vanish and do not return to the treasury. They roll to cohort 02 at the same dollar figure, reconciled seat by seat in the epoch report. Unclaimed value stays in the program.

10.10 A forfeited seat's 25% is not re-granted to anyone in cohort 01. The figure moves forward whole.

10.11 Abandoned streams continue vesting after cutoff and remain claimable indefinitely by the original address. Forfeiture affects the unvested portion only where a re-evaluation under §12 voids the leaf.

§11

Restricted regions

11.1 Twenty-two regions are excluded from cohort 01. Group A is sanctions-driven and is unlikely to change. Group B is licensing-driven and is expected to shrink — cohort 02 is where most of it clears.

11.2 Exclusion is from the seat, not from the network. A restricted address can still use, stake and swap; it simply cannot claim in cohort 01.

§12

Appeals and corrections

12.1 Any clause result, score, cluster assignment or region determination can be appealed. Grounds are narrow by design: the evaluator read the wrong state, the graph joined the wrong edge, the region fell back to IP wrongly, or the score arithmetic does not reproduce.

12.2 An appeal states the clause, the address, the block height of the state it relies on, and the corrected value. Without all four it is returned unread.

12.3 "I think I deserve more" is not a ground. "Tenure is 91 days at block 23,914,806, hash 0x…, your evaluator returned 88" is.

12.4 Filing window. Appeals on the live projection open 12 Nov 14:00 UTC and close at the snapshot. Appeals on the published root open at 12 Nov 06:00 and close 12 Nov 22:00 UTC, eight hours into the claim window. Acknowledgement within 24 hours, decision within 96. A decided appeal can move a leaf before it is claimed.

12.5 Appeals are decided by a three-person panel drawn from the seven signers, none of whom evaluated the address. Two of three carry. The panel's reasoning is published with the address redacted to the first four characters.

12.6 A successful appeal that would unseat another address triggers re-ranking inside the tier, not a 601st seat. Total stays 600.

12.7 Where we find an error unprompted, we correct it the same way and publish it in the amendment log whether or not anyone appealed.

Rough expectationOn a pool this size we expect 15–30 appeals, most of them the graph and the region fallback. We are publishing the expected number so that a low count later reads as a working function rather than a quiet one.
§13

Amendment and finality

13.1 Before the snapshot, this page can be amended. Every amendment is versioned, dated, and logged below with a link to the diff. A silent edit is a breach of these rules.

13.2 At the snapshot, version 1.0 freezes. Nothing after it changes a clause, a weight, a threshold or a seat count for cohort 01. Later versions govern later cohorts only.

13.3 Three things survive the freeze: appeal decisions under §12, corrections of our own errors under §12.7, and region reclassification under §11 only where a Group A addition is required by sanctions and affects no more than two seats.

13.4 The instrument is hashed at freeze. The SHA-256 of this document at version 1.0 is published in the footer and in the epoch report. Compare it.

§14

General provisions

14.1 Not a security. SPHERE pays for fees, stake and settlement. No equity, no dividend, no claim on assets, no redemption right against Spheres Labs Ltd.

14.2 Not advice. Nothing here is financial, legal or tax advice. An allocation is a dollar figure settled in USDT; SPHERE is a utility token with no claim on assets, and the two are not convertible at claim.

14.3 Tax. You are taxable where you are taxable. We issue no 1099, no equivalent, and no withholding. Record the claim block height; the receipt under §10.7 gives it to you.

14.4 Sanctions. A restricted-region determination is a program decision, not a legal one. If you are sanctions-adjacent and we got it wrong, §12 is the route.

14.5 Governing law. Cayman Islands, exclusive jurisdiction. Disputes go to the Grand Court of the Cayman Islands; the rules themselves are decided under §12 first.

14.6 Severability. A clause held void is severed; the rest survives. Where a severed clause changes arithmetic, the nearest integer equivalent applies and it is logged.

14.7 Good faith, stated plainly. These rules exist so that a stranger can predict an outcome without asking us. Where they fail at that, the failure is ours to fix in public, not yours to absorb.
