Privacy notice

Most of what we know about you is already public on a chain. This notice is about the small remainder. We never hold a key, a seed phrase or a balance — the exposure is wallet addresses, an attestation and whatever you write to us.

Controller: Spheres Labs Ltd. · George Town, Grand Cayman · walletsecurities@gmail.com

The short version. A wallet address, read-only public history, one attestation, and your email if you contact us. No keys. No KYC in cohort 01 unless you use the fiat ramp. No advertising cookies, no broker, no resale. Deleted 24 months after the epoch closes — except the published report, which is permanent.

01

Who controls your data

1.1

Spheres Labs Ltd., George Town, Grand Cayman KY1-1104, Cayman Islands, is the controller for the processing described here.

1.2

One address handles everything, including privacy requests: walletsecurities@gmail.com. No separate data protection officer is appointed, because the processing is narrow enough that one is not required; the same address is the escalation point.

1.3

This notice covers spheres.xyz and the Epoch 07 registration and claim flow. It does not cover third-party sites reached from here.

1.4

It sits alongside the terms, which govern the agreement itself. Where the two touch the same fact, they say the same thing.

02

What we collect

Nothing here is collected by default. Each item has a trigger you control.

2.1

Wallet address. Collected when you register or connect. This is the identifier the seat binds to.

2.2

Public chain history. Read, not copied wholesale: transaction count, first-activity date, distinct contracts touched, median volume, funding relationships. All of it is already public.

2.3

Attestation. Either an on-chain attestation reference or a social handle, collected if you take that path. Where you take the on-chain path it carries a jurisdiction claim — the country, not a document. See §3.

2.4

Email. Only if you write to us, and only what you put in the message.

2.5

Technical data. IP address truncated to network prefix (enough to resolve a country for the region fallback), user agent, browser language, page path, referrer. Kept as aggregate counts, not per-visitor profiles.

2.6

Claim payload. The signed typed data, the nonce, the digest and the transaction hash — needed to prove the claim and to rebuild it if it fails.

2.7

Publication. Your address may appear in the epoch report at §8.4. You should assume anything on-chain, including a claim, is permanently public.

2.8

Fiat partner data. If you use buy or sell, the licensed partner collects identity documents and payment details directly. We receive a status and an order id, not the documents.

03

What we never collect

Never

Private keys. Seed phrases. Keystore files. They stay in your wallet. Signing happens locally and only the output reaches us.

Never

Advertising identifiers, cross-site trackers, data brokers. No pixels, no retargeting, nothing sold.

Never in cohort 01

Document images. Attestation carries a jurisdiction claim, so a passport can clear the region gate without a scan ever reaching us. Scans exist only inside the fiat partner's own flow, if you choose to use it.

Never

Your balance as a stored figure. It is read at the moment of use, not warehoused.

The distinction in the third panel is worth stating plainly: we hold a country, not a copy of the document that proves it. If you attest a second passport to clear a Group B restriction, that is a second country value, not a second upload.

04

Why, and on what basis

4.1

To evaluate and score your wallet — performance of the participation agreement. Basis: contract.

4.2

To allocate a seat, fix the merkle leaf and let you claim — contract.

4.3

To detect sybil clusters and abuse — legitimate interest in 600 seats going to 600 real operators.

4.4

To answer you — contract, or legitimate interest where you write before registering.

4.5

To publish the epoch telemetry report — legitimate interest in the report being the product, and consent where an address is attributed.

4.6

To meet legal obligations — legal obligation, principally sanctions screening at the region gate.

4.7

We do not carry out automated decision-making with legal effect beyond the deterministic clause evaluation described in the rules, which you can read in full.

05

Cookies and local storage

5.1

No advertising or analytics cookies. There is nothing to consent to and therefore no banner.

5.2

Local storage holds three things only: a cached seat count, a cached claim intent so a claim survives a page reload, and a short session marker that expires after 30 minutes. All are cleared when you clear site data.

5.3

Web fonts are fetched from Google Fonts, which sees the request. No other third-party asset loads on a page view.

06

Who else sees it

6.1

Hosting and RPC providers — serve the site and read chain state. Process on our instructions.

6.2

The relayer — receives the signed payload in order to submit it. Sees the payload, not your key.

6.3

Licensed fiat partners — only if you use buy or sell. They are controllers in their own right for the KYC they perform.

6.4

Public block explorers — anything on-chain is public by construction. PolygonScan and Etherscan are linked, not embedded.

6.5

Auditors and counsel — under confidentiality, where needed to verify the flow.

6.6

Nobody else. No data broker, no marketing platform, no resale.

07

International transfers

7.1

We are in the Cayman Islands and use providers in the EU, the UK and the United States. Data therefore leaves your jurisdiction.

7.2

Transfers rely on standard contractual clauses or an adequacy finding, depending on the destination.

7.3

The volume is small — an address, a handle, a payload — so the practical risk is correspondingly narrow.

08

How long it is kept

8.1

Registration, score and claim record — until 24 months after the epoch closes, then deleted. Kept that long to answer a cluster correction or a duplicate-claim dispute.

8.2

Technical data — 90 days raw, then aggregate indefinitely.

8.3

Correspondence — 24 months.

8.4

Telemetry report — published permanently. Addresses inside it are already public and are not withdrawn on erasure. This is the one retention period that outlives the 24 months, and it is deliberate.

8.5

Fiat records — held by the partner under their own retention, typically five years for financial records.

09

Security

9.1

AES-256 at rest, TLS in transit, per-epoch key rotation.

9.2

Reserve funds sit behind a 4-of-7 multisig with hardware-held keys across three jurisdictions. That is custody, not personal data, but it is the same posture.

9.3

Access to registration data is limited to those who need it to run the epoch, and is logged.

9.4

A breach affecting personal data is notified to the regulator where required, and to affected participants by email or on X where we hold no address for them.

9.5

No system is perfect. A wrong address in a claim is unrecoverable regardless of our security, because the chain does not arbitrate.

10

Your rights

10.1

You may ask for access to what we hold, correction of it, erasure, restriction, portability, or object to processing based on legitimate interest.

10.2

Request by writing to walletsecurities@gmail.com from the address you registered, or by signing a short message from the registered wallet. A signature from the wallet is the strongest proof and needs no email.

10.3

We answer within 30 days. Extension is possible where a request is complex, and we will say so before the deadline.

10.4

Erasure is limited in three ways, and we will tell you which applies: an active claim needs its address record until it settles; the published telemetry report is permanent; on-chain data cannot be erased at all.

10.5

Withdrawing consent stops future processing but does not unwind a claim already made.

11

Children

11.1

The epoch is for those 18 and over. We do not knowingly process data of anyone younger.

11.2

If a minor registers with a parent's wallet, the parent is treated as the participant.

12

On-chain data and immutability

12.1

Anything written to a chain — the claim, the allowance, the transfer — is permanent and world-readable. Erasure rights do not reach it.

12.2

That permanence is a feature, not an oversight: it is what lets a third party verify your allocation without trusting this page.

12.3

An address is pseudonymous, not anonymous. Where it links to a handle or an email it becomes identifying. We drop the link at 24 months under §8.1, but a reader of the published report can still join the two themselves — so treat the link as durable, not as expiring.

13

Changes

13.1

Material changes are announced on X at least 72 hours before they take effect and the version number above is incremented.

13.2

Changes apply from publication. They do not retrospectively widen what was collected before.

14

Contact and complaints

14.1

Any privacy question or request: walletsecurities@gmail.com.

14.2

If we get it wrong, tell us first — most of it is fixable in one reply.

14.3

You may also complain to the Cayman Islands Ombudsman, or to the supervisory authority where you live.
