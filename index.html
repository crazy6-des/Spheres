02 — The number

Your figure is in dollars. It is paid in dollars.

Most pools price an allocation in their own token and hand you the spot risk that comes with it — the number shrinks the day the chart does, and the promise was never about your figure, only about theirs. Here the tier amount is denominated in USD and paid in USDT. There is no conversion step, no reference rate to drift and nothing to sell on the way in.

The mean across the pool is $5,000. Where you land inside it depends on tenure first, then how many distinct contracts you have used, then median volume — in that order, so an old quiet wallet beats a new loud one. All of it is the whole pool; nothing is carved out for the team.

04 — Rationale

Why a chain pays strangers to use it

Every network ships after testing against synthetic load, and every network is wrong. Benchmarks are written by people who know the spec — no failed swaps, no stuck approvals, no one bridging at 3am into a congested pool. The only load that matters is load from people who did not read the spec.

Six hundred seats is small enough to audit every wallet by hand and large enough to saturate the sequencer at a realistic mix. Payouts are funded and locked before the first claim opens, so the incentive sits on-chain instead of in a promise. When the epoch closes, the telemetry publishes in full — including the blocks we lost. That report is the actual product of this epoch; the pool is what it costs.

Purpose

Real mix, not synthetic

Swaps, stakes, bridges and failed transactions from 600 independent operators. Adversarial by construction.

Reason

We publish the failures

The telemetry report ships whether it flatters us or not. Lost blocks are listed by height.

Bound

Four cohorts, then open

600 → 1,200 → 2,400 → permissionless. Each doubles until the sequencer holds. This one is deliberately small.

05 — Custody

Reserves on-chain before claims open

The full $3,000,000 in USDT is deposited and locked 72 hours before the epoch opens, at an address published in the docs and mirrored in the footer. Readable by anyone with an explorer. There is no "we will fund it as claims arrive" step.

Locking

4-of-7 multisig, HSM-held

Seven signers across three jurisdictions, hardware-backed, four required to move anything. No single operator can touch the pool.

Vesting

Quarter at claim, rest linear

25% immediately, 75% over 90 days. Discourages claim-and-dump and keeps the load test running past the deadline.

Recycling

Unclaimed seats do not vanish

Anything left at cutoff returns to cohort 02. Reconciled seat by seat in the epoch report.

Binding

Claim binds to the scored address

The merkle leaf is the address read at the snapshot. Claiming elsewhere is not reversible — check twice.

10 — Questions

Before you ask

Do I need to buy anything?
No. No deposit, no purchase, no bridge. You need gas for one approve — about nine cents. If you hold any balance on the chain at all, you already have enough.
What does the approval actually let through?
Up to your allocation of USDT, to the Permit2 contract, for thirty days. Nothing else. Every other token sits outside the allowance.
Can I revoke it?
Any time, without affecting the claim. Or leave it — it expires on its own at thirty days.
What if the price falls?
Nothing to fall. You are paid in USDT, so the figure in the tier table is the figure that arrives. No conversion, no reference rate, no spot exposure between registering and claiming.
Why only 600?
Six hundred is what our team can audit by hand before the snapshot, and small enough that one farming cluster cannot distort the data. Cohort 02 doubles to 1,200.
When do I get it?
25% at claim, the rest streaming over ninety days, in USDT. Claim opens 12 Nov 14:00 UTC.
Is this a security?
No. SPHERE pays for fees, stake and settlement. No equity, no dividend, no claim on assets. US persons sit out cohort 01 while counsel finishes.
Where can I check the contracts myself?
Every address is labelled on PolygonScan as a Sphere Finance contract and every audit is public: SPHERE token · USDT · CertiK · SourceHat · Cyberscope.
