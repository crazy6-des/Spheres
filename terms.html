Terms of participation

These terms govern Epoch 07: who may register, how a wallet is scored, how an allocation is fixed, and what you actually sign at the claim. Read §7 before you approve anything — it is the only step where you give up a permission.

Spheres Labs Ltd. · George Town, Grand Cayman · walletsecurities@gmail.com

The short version. Cohort 01 is closed to US persons and twenty-one other regions. Your allocation is a dollar figure, settled in USDT, and the pool is deposited before claims open. You pay about nine cents of gas once; everything after that is a free signature. Nothing here is equity, a dividend or a claim on assets.

02

Definitions

2.1

"Epoch" means one load-testing cohort. Epoch 07 is Cohort 01 of four and is the only cohort these terms cover.

2.2

"Snapshot block" means block 23,914,806. "Snapshot" means the state of public chain history at that height.

2.3

"Clauses" means the five pass-or-fail tests at §3 of the rules. "Score" means the integer, maximum 1,000, produced by the ranking function at §4 of the rules. Clauses decide whether you may be seated; the score decides where.

2.4

"Allocation" means the USD figure attached to a seat in a tier. It is settled in USDT.

2.5

"SPHERE" means the token at 0x62F594339830b90AE4C084aE7D223fFAFd9658A7 on Polygon, and any bridged representation of the same asset. SPHERE is not the settlement asset of an allocation under these terms.

2.6

"Permit2" means the canonical contract at 0x000000000022D473030F116dDEE9F6B43aC78BA3.

2.7

"Arm" means granting a capped, expiring USDT allowance to Permit2. "Claim" means the two-part act of arming and then signing typed data that releases your allocation.

03

Eligibility

3.1

A wallet is eligible only if, at the snapshot block, all five clauses at §3 of the rules return true: tenure of at least 90 days, at least five settled transactions across two or more verified contracts, no losing cluster membership, an identity attestation, and a permitted region.

3.2

Evaluation is deterministic against the snapshot. There is no discretionary review at first instance; §12 of the rules is the appeal route.

3.3

One seat per cluster, not per wallet. Where several wallets group into one funding cluster, the highest scoring wallet takes the seat and the others are shadowed — eligible, but seated only if the winner does not claim.

3.4

Failure of clause four caps you at Tier 3 rather than excluding you. Failure of any other clause excludes you.

3.5

A seat is personal to the scored address and is not transferable before it is claimed.

04

Registration and attestation

4.1

Registration records the address you nominate. The address must be one you control; we do not verify control beyond the claim signature.

4.2

Attestation is satisfied by an on-chain attestation or by a verified social handle. A handle is verified once and bound to the cluster.

4.3

You may not register an address you do not control, or an address already registered by someone else for the same epoch.

4.4

Registering does not guarantee a seat. Seats are allocated in score order until 600 are filled; the remainder wait for cohort 02.

05

Snapshot and scoring

5.1

Scores freeze at block 23,914,806 on 11 December 2026 at 23:59 UTC. Activity after that height does not change your tier.

5.2

Ranking inputs are applied in fixed order: tenure, then distinct contracts used, then median transaction volume. An older quieter wallet outranks a newer louder one by construction.

5.3

Only settled transactions count. Self-transfers and reverted transactions do not.

5.4

If a chain reorganisation below the snapshot height changes the result, the snapshot is re-read at the new canonical state and the published tier list is corrected within 24 hours.

06

Allocation and settlement

6.1

Tier amounts are denominated in USD: $15,000 at T1, $6,000 at T2, $1,000 at T3. The mean across the pool is $5,000.

6.2

The figure is settled in USDT at the figure itself. There is no conversion step and no reference rate applied at claim. The $0.42 mark is an accounting input used to provision the reserve, not a price applied to your allocation.

6.3

If SPHERE closes below $0.30 on a 30-day TWAP before the claim window opens, the treasury tops the pool back to par in USDT. The top-up is a funding commitment, not a price guarantee after the window opens.

6.4

The full $3,000,000 is deposited and locked 72 hours before the window opens. You may read the balance before the window opens.

6.5

The merkle leaf binds your allocation to the scored address. There is no redirect: a claim from any other address fails.

6.6

Once released, USDT is USDT. If you want SPHERE — to stake or to hold — you swap for it at the router and take the market from there.

07

Claim mechanics and the approval

7.1

A claim is two acts. First a one-time ERC-20 approval of USDT to Permit2, costing roughly nine cents of gas. Second a gasless typed-data signature.

7.2

The claim contract requires an armed allowance before it will accept a signature. It is a gate, not a payment: nothing is pulled at claim. The allowance is capped at your allocation and expires on-chain after 30 days.

7.3

The cap is a ceiling on what the spender may ever move out of your wallet — it is a permission over your USDT up to that figure, not a debit, and not a ceiling on what we send you. Your other tokens and any USDT above the cap sit outside it.

7.4

The signature carries a single-use nonce and a deadline. Once submitted the nonce is spent and the signature cannot be replayed.

7.5

You sign with your own wallet. We never hold your keys, your seed phrase or a copy of your private key.

7.6

The relayer submits the transaction and pays its gas. The relayer cannot alter the amount, the recipient or the deadline, because all three are inside the signed payload.

7.7

You may revoke the allowance at any time without affecting a claim already made. It also expires unaided.

7.8

If the approval lands but the signature is not submitted before its deadline, the claim fails and must be re-signed. The allowance remains until it expires or is revoked.

08

Vesting

8.1

25% of an allocation is spendable the moment the claim transaction confirms.

8.2

The remaining 75% streams linearly over 90 days from confirmation, in USDT. Unvested allocation is held in escrow and is not transferable.

8.3

Unlocked balance is exposed separately from escrow, so partially vested allocation can be sold, swapped or staked without waiting for the full period.

8.4

Vesting runs on wall-clock time and is unaffected by whether you interact with the network during the period.

09

Custody of reserves

9.1

The pool is held by a 4-of-7 multisig across seven signers in three jurisdictions, hardware-backed. Four signatures are required to move anything.

9.2

Two signers are external to the company and none hold two keys.

9.3

No single operator, including any founder, can move the pool alone.

9.4

The reserve address is published in the docs and mirrored in the site footer, and is readable on a public explorer.

9.5

Nothing in the pool is lent, deployed to a yield venue or rehypothecated while the window is open.

10

Sybil clusters and abuse

10.1

Clustering is by funding graph. Wallets funded from a common source within a short window group together.

10.2

Where a cluster is found after a claim, we may recover the excess allocation of the lower-scoring members and return it to the pool. Excess already vested is not clawed back.

10.3

Deliberate score manipulation — wash volume, contract-deployment padding, attestation reuse across clusters — may be corrected at the snapshot evaluation stage.

10.4

Corrections are published with the tier list, not applied silently.

Nothing on this site is investment, legal, accounting or tax advice, and no communication from us creates a fiduciary duty.

14

Prohibited conduct

14.1

You may not register or claim where doing so breaches the law of your residence.

14.2

You may not operate a farm, botnet or delegated-claim service that presents many wallets as one participant, or one participant as many.

14.3

You may not attack the site, the claim contract, the relayer or the sequencer, or attempt to replay a spent signature.

14.4

You may not claim on behalf of another person without their authority over the signing key.

Telemetry and publication

15.1

The point of the epoch is the report. We publish transaction telemetry in full within 96 hours of cutoff, including failed transactions and lost blocks, listed by height.

15.2

The report is published whether or not it flatters the network.

15.3

You grant us a perpetual licence to publish aggregate telemetry derived from your participation. Wallet addresses may appear in the report; they are already public on-chain.

15.4

Personal data handling is set out separately in the privacy notice.

16

Unclaimed seats

16.1

A seat not claimed by 12 December 2026 at 14:00 UTC is released and returned to cohort 02.

16.2

Release is not compensable. There is no credit, no carry-over and no priority in the next cohort.

16.3

Released seats are reconciled one by one in the epoch report.

19

Force majeure and chain events

19.1

We are not in breach while prevented by events outside reasonable control: sequencer halt, consensus failure, bridge compromise, sustained RPC outage, act of state.

19.2

Where such an event prevents claims, the window is suspended and re-opened with at least 24 hours' notice rather than allowed to expire.

19.3

A chain reorganisation deeper than the snapshot is treated as a chain event, not as a scoring dispute.

20.3

If a clause is held unenforceable, the rest survives and the clause is read down to the minimum enforceable form.

20.4

No failure to enforce is a waiver.
